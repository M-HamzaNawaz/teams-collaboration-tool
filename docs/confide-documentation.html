How to read this document

Sections 1–6 are for anyone. Sections 7–12 go into technical detail for engineering review. Section 13 is deliberately candid about what the product does not do — that section matters most in a client conversation, because it is the difference between a promise and a commitment.

1. What Confide is, and the problem it solves

A messaging platform for agencies working with clients, where the messaging is the visible part and the protection is the product.

The problem

An agency introduces a client to a contractor, or a specialist, or a team member. They collaborate in a shared chat. Then someone shares a phone number or an email address, the conversation moves to WhatsApp, and the next project is arranged without the agency in the room.

This is the single largest revenue leak in agency work, and it is invisible: by the time it is noticed, the relationship has already moved. Ordinary chat tools cannot help, because from their point of view nothing unusual happened — someone simply sent a message.

What Confide does

Confide is a group chat platform where every message is inspected by the server before anyone can read it. If a message contains contact information — a phone number, an email address, a WhatsApp link, a payment handle — it does not get delivered. It waits for an administrator to approve or block it, and the decision is recorded permanently.

The core guarantee

A message containing contact information cannot reach its recipient unless a human administrator releases it. This is not enforced by the app politely declining to send — it is enforced by the database, which does not grant the browser permission to create a message at all.

What that gives the agency

  • Prevention — contact details are stopped before they arrive, not flagged after the fact.
  • Visibility — the administrator sees every attempt, including the ones they approve.
  • Evidence — a permanent, tamper-evident record of who tried to share what, and when. This is what makes a non-circumvention agreement enforceable rather than decorative.
  • Identity control — clients see the names the agency assigns, not the personal identities of the people doing the work.

What the client experiences

An ordinary, modern chat application. Groups, replies, file sharing, formatting, notifications, read receipts. The screening is invisible unless it triggers — and when it does, the sender sees a clear "pending review" marker on their own message rather than a silent failure.

2. The three guarantees

These are not features that could be switched off or bypassed by a bug in the application. They are enforced by the database itself.

Guarantee 1 — No private messages. Groups only.

There is no direct-message feature, and there is no way to add one without redesigning the database. The structure cannot represent a conversation between exactly two people. Only administrators can create groups.

Why it matters: a private channel between two members is precisely where an exchange of contact details would happen unobserved. Removing the possibility is stronger than monitoring it.

Guarantee 2 — Identity is controlled by the agency.

Members cannot change their own display name. When an administrator invites someone, the administrator sets the name the others will see. A member who wants a different name submits a request, which an administrator reviews.

Why it matters: without this, a member could simply rename themselves to their phone number or email address and bypass the entire screening system in a single move.

Guarantee 3 — Archive is not deletion, and the record is permanent.

Groups are archived, never erased. The audit log physically rejects any attempt to modify or delete an entry — including from the application's own administrative account. Each entry is cryptographically linked to the one before it, so removing or altering a single line breaks a chain that the system verifies nightly.

Why it matters: evidence that can be quietly edited is not evidence. This is what allows the record to be handed to a lawyer.

Why enforce at the database level

Application code changes constantly, and every change is a chance to introduce a mistake. These three rules are enforced one layer below the application, so a bug in a screen, a route or a future feature cannot violate them. Each is covered by an automated test that runs on every change.

3. How message screening works

The detection engine is the component the entire product rests on. It reads text and decides one of three things.

OutcomeWhat happens
AllowDelivered immediately. Nothing recorded beyond the ordinary message record.
FlagDelivered immediately, but the finding is recorded for the administrator. Used where something was detected but is very unlikely to be a genuine attempt — an automated email from a service, for example.
HoldNot delivered. Waits in the moderation queue. The recipient sees nothing at all until an administrator approves it.
Why three outcomes and not two

A system that only blocks or allows must choose between missing real attempts and freezing ordinary work. The middle outcome is what makes the product usable: a pasted error log containing a service email address is recorded but delivered, while a personal address is held.

What it detects

Twenty-eight separate detection rules across five categories:

CategoryExamples caught
Phone numbersInternational (+92 300 1234567), national (03001234567), spaced digits, digits spelled out in words, numbers glued to text
Email addressesStandard, and disguised forms — john [at] gmail [dot] com, john@gmail,com, j o h n @ …
Messaging linksWhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Instagram, Snapchat, Discord, Skype, LinkedIn, Facebook
Payment handlesPayPal.me, Wise, Payoneer, IBAN bank details
Crypto walletsBitcoin (both formats), Ethereum, Tron

Where the check happens

Screening runs on the server, before a message row exists. It is not a check the app performs on its way to the database — it is a gate the message must pass to become a message at all. A modified client, a direct API call or a replayed request all arrive at the same place, so there is no route into a conversation that skips it.

3a. How the engine resists evasion

Anyone determined to pass a number through will try to disguise it. The engine runs in layers, each closing a different class of disguise.

The five stages, in order

StageWhat it does
1. NormaliseText is reduced to a canonical form: look-alike letters from other alphabets become their Latin equivalents, and invisible characters are stripped.
2. MatchAll twenty-eight rules run over both the normalised text and the raw original, so a trick that survives one view is caught in the other.
3. MergeOverlapping findings of the same kind collapse to the strongest one.
4. AllowlistKnown-harmless senders drop from hold to flag: delivered, still recorded.
5. DecideEach finding carries a confidence score. At or above the hold threshold the message stops; below it, it delivers flagged.

Defeating disguises

A Cyrillic "о" in an email address, or a zero-width space inside a phone number, is removed before matching. Formatting is stripped and the message re-checked, so a number split with bold markers still reads as one number. Letters standing in for digits — o for zero, l for one — are folded back inside runs that are mostly digits.

Phone numbers are validated, not just matched

A run of digits is checked against the international numbering standard to see whether it is a real number in any country, using per-country length and prefix rules. A bare number from Pakistan, the UAE, the UK or India is caught with no configuration; an invoice or order reference of the same length is not.

The allowlist is not a blind spot

Seventeen tooling domains — GitHub, Stripe, Figma, Sentry — and machine senders such as noreply@ are demoted rather than held, because they appear constantly in real work. Free consumer providers are deliberately absent: that is where contact exchange happens. A demoted finding is still recorded.

Measured accuracy

The engine is tested against a corpus of real and adversarial examples on every change, and every evasion found joins that corpus permanently, so the same trick cannot work twice. Two thresholds are enforced automatically, and a change breaching either cannot be merged:

3b. Screening strictness, per group

Different rooms need different rules. A client-facing room and an internal team room should not be screened identically.

An administrator sets the level from the group's menu. It applies from the next message, with no technical work required.

LevelHoldsOrdinary messages held
Contact info only
default		Phone numbers, emails, messaging links, payment handles, crypto addressesNone measured
Long numbers tooThe above, plus any number of seven digits or moreAbout 28%
Any number at allThe above, plus every digit — including "3pm" and "v2.0.1"About 67%
Recommendation

Use the strictest level on client-facing rooms only. The percentages above are measured, not estimated — at the strictest setting roughly two-thirds of ordinary conversation is held for review, which is appropriate for a room where no number should ever be exchanged and unworkable for an internal team room.

What cannot be switched off

The settings control whether a finding holds a message. They cannot disable detection itself, and they cannot disable the audit trail. Contact information is always detected and always recorded; a group may only choose whether it waits for review or is delivered with a note.

4. Roles and who can do what

RoleCan doCannot do
Administrator
the agency		 Create groups, invite people, set display names, approve or block held messages, set screening strictness, archive and delete groups, read the full audit trail and export it Modify or delete audit entries. Delete a group without archiving it first.

Client Read and write in their groups, share files Everything a member cannot, plus: see other members' internal nicknames, and see the moderation queue under any circumstances

What each person sees of the others

A visibility layer decides field by field what one role may see about another. A client sees display name, role label and avatar. Team members see those plus internal nicknames. Real email addresses and phone numbers are stored in a table that the application's ordinary read permissions cannot access at all — not filtered in code, but unreadable at the database level.

One group manager

A group may have a designated manager who can approve or block held messages for that group only. A client can never hold this role, because the moderation queue displays exactly the contact details the holds exist to contain.

Removal takes effect in seconds

When someone is removed from a group or workspace, their existing session is revoked rather than left to expire. Access tokens are short-lived by design, so a removed person cannot continue reading past that window even if they keep the application open.

5. The moderation workflow

1
Someone sends a message. It goes to the server. The browser has no permission to write a message directly — this is enforced by database privileges, not by application code.
2
The server screens it. The detection engine runs before any message record exists. Formatting is stripped and it is screened a second time, so disguises using bold or italics do not work.
3
Clean messages are delivered instantly. They appear for everyone in the group in real time.
4
Flagged messages are held. The sender sees "pending review" on their own message. Other members see nothing — not a placeholder, not a gap. The message does not exist for them.
5
Administrators are alerted. Their dashboard updates live, and an email is sent as a fallback if no administrator is currently online.
6
An administrator decides. They see the message with the detected text highlighted exactly as it was typed, and choose approve or block. Both outcomes are recorded permanently with who decided and how long it took.
7
Approved messages are delivered in their original position in the conversation, marked "released after review" so they are not missed by someone who has already scrolled past.

If nobody responds

AfterWhat happens
30 minutesThe held message escalates to the group manager. Recorded in the audit trail as a system action.
8 hoursThe message is released automatically, still flagged and still fully recorded, and marked as auto-released rather than approved by a person.

Both intervals are configurable per group. The reasoning for automatic release is that a chat tool which silently swallows messages overnight will not be used — and a tool that is not used protects nothing. The release is visible and attributed, never silent.

6. The three platforms

One product, one codebase, three ways to reach it. Everything below shares the same screening, the same data and the same audit trail.

The architectural decision behind all three

The application itself is a web application. The desktop and mobile apps are native windows wrapping that same web application — the identical approach Slack and Discord use. The consequence is significant: every improvement ships to all three platforms at once, with no app-store review and no reinstall. Only changes to native behaviour require a new build, which has happened roughly ten times in the project's life.

The product itself. Everything else is a window onto this.

A modern web application that runs in any current browser on any device. There is nothing to install, updates are instant, and there is no version fragmentation — everyone is always on the current release.

A native desktop application — its own icon, its own window, its own place in the taskbar or dock. Inside that window runs the same web application, which is why it never falls behind: the moment a change is deployed, the desktop app has it.

  • Unread badge — on the dock icon (macOS) or taskbar (Windows), showing what needs attention.

Release process

Publishing a version is a single tagged commit. An automated pipeline builds all three operating systems, signs the update artefacts and publishes the installers; installed apps find the release in minutes.

7a. How the pieces fit together

Three architectural decisions do most of the work. Each moves a guarantee out of the application code, where a bug could break it, and into a layer that cannot be talked out of it.

The path a message takes

 Step
1Someone types a message — in a browser, or in the desktop or Android app, which run the same application.
2It is sent to a server route. The browser is not permitted to write it directly.
3The route establishes who is asking and whether they may write to that group.
4The detection engine reads the text and returns allow, flag or hold.
5Only then is a row written, with that outcome already attached to it.
6Recipients receive it over a live connection that carries only what the database's own rules permit each person to see.

Two database identities, deliberately unequal

The browser connects with an identity that may read messages and nothing more. The server connects with a separate, privileged identity kept in a file that fails the build if it is ever imported into browser code. A leak of that key would expose every workspace, so the compiler prevents it rather than developer discipline.

One authorization choke point

Because the privileged identity bypasses the database's own row rules, every route that uses it first calls a single authorization function covering thirteen named actions — send a message, moderate one, add a member, archive a group, and so on. There is one place to read, one place to test, and no second route with its own interpretation of who may do what.

A request aimed at another agency's workspace is answered "not found" rather than "not allowed", because confirming that a group exists is itself a disclosure.

Why the browser cannot write messages

Every message must pass through a server route, and that route runs the detection engine before writing anything. The guarantee therefore does not depend on the application behaving correctly: someone with a valid login, developer tools open and full knowledge of the system still cannot insert a message that skipped screening — the database will refuse it.

8. Security and data protection

Separation between workspaces

Every table carries a workspace identifier and every access rule filters through it. An automated test generates the list of tables from the database itself and verifies, for each one, that a user in one workspace can read nothing belonging to another. A new table ships with this coverage or the build fails — there is no hand-maintained list to forget to update.

Contact details are unreadable, not merely hidden

Real email addresses and phone numbers live in a table the application's ordinary read permissions cannot access at all. This is not a filter applied in code that could be forgotten on one screen; the permission does not exist.

Files

  • Stored in private storage. No file has a public address.
  • Downloads are authorised individually and given a link valid for five minutes.
  • Filenames are screened exactly like message text — a file named with a phone number holds the message carrying it.
  • A virus-scanning step sits in the upload path, ready for a scanner to be connected.

Sign-in and sessions

  • Email and password, with rate limiting per account and per network address.
  • Sign-in responses are deliberately identical whether or not the account exists, so the login form cannot be used to discover who has an account.
  • Access tokens last ten minutes and renew invisibly, so removing someone takes effect within that window rather than whenever they happen to sign out.
  • Invitation links are single-use, expire after seven days, and the link itself is never stored — only an irreversible fingerprint of it.

Logging discipline

No message content, email address or phone number is written to server logs. A product whose value is confidentiality cannot leak conversation into a third-party logging service, so log entries carry identifiers only.

Recorded identity events

Sign-in, sign-out and password changes are all recorded in the permanent audit trail — so the question "who could have been in this account, and from when" has an answer.

9. The audit trail

The feature that turns a policy into something enforceable.

What is recorded

Every action of consequence: messages sent, held, approved, blocked, escalated and auto-released; groups created, archived and deleted; people invited, added and removed; names changed and name requests reviewed; files uploaded; sign-ins, sign-outs and password changes; screening settings changed, with both the previous and new values.

Why it cannot be altered

  • The database rejects any attempt to modify or delete an entry — including from the application's own administrative account.
  • Each entry carries a cryptographic fingerprint of the entry before it, forming a chain. Changing or removing any line breaks every fingerprint after it.
  • The chain is verified automatically every night, and the result is shown on the dashboard. An administrator can also verify it on demand.

Why entries survive deletion

Audit entries store the names of people and groups as plain text copies rather than references. When a group is permanently deleted or a person leaves, the record still reads correctly years later. An entry that renders as "<deleted> did something in <deleted>" is not evidence.

Export for a dispute

The full timeline exports to a spreadsheet file, with three levels of detail chosen at export time:

LevelContains
Timeline onlyWho did what, and when. No message content.
Plus findingsAdds the exact text that was detected — the phone number or address itself. This is usually the fact a dispute turns on.
Plus full messagesAdds the complete message text.

Exports that include message content are themselves recorded in the audit trail, naming who exported, how much, and what was included. An evidence surface that cannot say who copied it is a weaker one.

10. Notifications

Three delivery routes, because no single one covers every situation.

RouteWhen it appliesWhere it works
In-pageThe application is open and visibleEverywhere
Operating systemThe desktop app is running, possibly minimisedDesktop app

How it behaves

  • One control turns notifications on or off, and it governs all routes.
  • Only one route fires per message — no duplicate alerts.
  • Notification content respects the visibility rules: recipients see the display name they are permitted to see, not the sender's real identity.
  • Held messages notify nobody. A message waiting for review has not been delivered, and a notification would defeat the hold entirely.
  • Sound and an unread badge accompany the alert where the platform supports them.

Administrator alerts

When a message is held, administrator dashboards update live. If no administrator is currently online, an email is sent instead. Both are best-effort — the hold itself is already permanent in the database, so a missed alert delays review but never loses a message.

What each layer checks

LayerWhat it proves
Unit testsIndividual pieces behave correctly — the detection engine, the permission function, text normalisation, invitation tokens.
Database rule testsThe three guarantees hold at the database level: workspace separation, no write permissions for browsers, the audit log rejecting changes, deletion rules.
Screening corpusAccuracy against real and adversarial examples, with a minimum catch rate and a maximum false-alarm rate enforced automatically.
Journey testsThe entire product working end to end in a real browser: invite someone, accept, send a phone number, confirm the sender sees "pending review", confirm the recipient receives nothing, approve it, confirm delivery, confirm the audit trail records the whole chain.
The test that matters most

One automated test performs the product's entire reason for existing: that a message containing a phone number reaches no recipient until an administrator approves it, and that every step is recorded. If that test fails, nothing ships.

The screening corpus, specifically

Two limits are enforced on every change: at least 95% of contact information must be caught, and at most 5% of ordinary messages may be wrongly held. Current measured performance is 100% caught and 0% wrongly held. Every evasion attempt found in real testing is added permanently, so the same technique cannot succeed twice.

2

3

Backups and recovery

  • Nightly database export covering structure and data.
  • Restore procedure documented and rehearsed rather than assumed.
  • Health checks and a written incident runbook.
  • Log discipline verified: a staged incident must be diagnosable from logs alone, with no message content present in them.

Database changes

Every structural change is a numbered, sequential file, never edited once merged. The entire database can be rebuilt from these files alone, which is what makes the restore rehearsal meaningful. Thirty such files exist today across fifteen tables.

13. Known limits and what comes next

Stated deliberately. Every one of these is a considered decision rather than an oversight, and it is better for a client to hear them here than to discover one mid-pilot.

Current limits

LimitImpact and plan
Images are not read A phone number written inside a screenshot or photograph passes through untouched. This is realistically the most likely way to bypass the system. Solving it requires text recognition on images; not yet scheduled.
File contents are not read A number inside a document passes through. Filenames are screened. Planned for the next phase.
Notifications on the Android app while closed Requires an integration with Google's messaging service — free, roughly two to three days of work. The browser-installed version already handles this correctly.
Conversation outside the platform If two people are already speaking on a phone call, nothing here can prevent an exchange. This is accepted by design: the product makes bypassing high-friction and evidenced, not impossible.
Voice and video calls Not included in this phase, so no exposure through them yet. A later phase adds calls together with transcript screening.

Planned next

  • An intelligent screening pass — a language model reviewing what the current rules cannot judge: intent, unusual phrasing, and disguises no pattern anticipates. The existing rules stay as the fast path and the fallback.
  • File and image content screening, closing the largest current gap.
  • Notifications for the Android app while closed.
  • Project milestones and payments, keeping the commercial relationship on the platform as well as the conversation.
  • Voice and video calls with transcript screening.
The honest summary for a client

Confide makes sharing contact details inside the platform difficult, visible and permanently recorded. It does not make private contact between two determined people impossible — nothing can. What it does is remove the casual route, catch the deliberate one, and leave evidence in both cases. That is what makes a non-circumvention agreement something you can act on rather than something you merely signed.
